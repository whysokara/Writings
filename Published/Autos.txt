Autos feel like the most honest way to move through a city.

You never deliberate before getting into one. You do not evaluate comfort, image, or outcome. If an auto is there and you need to go somewhere, the decision is already made. That instinct is shared by people with very different lives.

For some, autos are the obvious choice. They are affordable, predictable, and available. For others, they are chosen even when other options exist. No doors. No glass. No separation from the street. It feels closer to the city.

Autos sit in a space most things fail to occupy. Too many things pick sides. Autos don’t. Like a plain T-shirt that works at home and still works when you step out. They are not aspirational or apologetic. They just function.

Inside, you carry no expectations. That is their biggest advantage. You do not wait for silence, cushioning, or temperature control. Nothing is promised, so nothing disappoints. The air comes in directly. So does the noise. You are not insulated from the city. You are moving with it.

And often, it carries more than people. Flowers stacked so high they lean into traffic. A car would hide them. An auto lets the street witness them.
Cars tend to look alike. Autos don’t. Every one carries its driver’s fingerprint. A favourite actor on the front or back, a sticker half peeled, a choice that belongs to the driver.

Most categories in life lack a middle ground. Homes signal status. Products announce price before purpose. Spaces decide who belongs before letting you in. Autos quietly avoid all of that.

If there had to be only one way to move around a city, something no one would strongly resist, this would survive. Not because it is perfect, because it asks very little from you.

Maybe every part of life needs something like this. A middle that does not judge. A default that feels usable, familiar, and natural, no matter where you stand.

Autos are not trying to be special.
They just happen to make sense.
