We spend our days in air-conditioned offices, surrounded by high-speed internet, ergonomic chairs, and coffee machines. We speak in polished tones, attend virtual meetings, and convince ourselves that this is who we are—corporate professionals, achievers.

But step outside, and the real world hits you. That sutta shop near your office, standing under the open sky, is just as much your reality. No AC, no fancy coffee—just chai in a flimsy cup, sipped while standing. The sun burns, the rain surprises, but no one complains.

Here, no one cares about your job title or unread emails. No KPIs, no HR policies—just unfiltered talk about cricket, politics, and life. It’s a reminder that the office doesn’t define you; it’s just a role you play.

But both worlds exist, and that’s okay. The office gives you structure, the sutta shop keeps you real. You don’t have to choose. You are both—the professional in meetings and the person sipping chai outside, where life is raw and real.

The office is where you work. The sutta shop is where you exist.
