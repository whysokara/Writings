No one remembered when the board first appeared. It had always been there, long before anyone learned how to move.

People discovered early that not all steps were the same. Most moved forward, one square at a time. That was considered normal. Safe. A few reached the far end and became something else. Those stories were told often, as proof that the board rewarded effort. The many who disappeared along the way were rarely mentioned.

Some people moved differently. They jumped. They landed where obstacles never seemed to matter. When asked how they did it, they never had a clear answer. They said it just worked.

Others moved only in straight lines, focused and unstoppable, unable to turn even when they wanted to. A few drifted diagonally, always slightly off, seeing paths that vanished if you tried to look at them directly.

At the center stood the most protected figure of all. Important enough that everyone adjusted around him. Powerful enough that the game paused if he was threatened. Yet they never moved far. One careful square at a time.

The strange part was how normal all of this felt. No one was being stopped. Some moves simply never worked.

People who tried to move differently felt it quickly. A pause. A wrong step. A quiet nudge back into place.

And sometimes, in the brief silence between moves, a thought surfaced.

Not about winning.
But about whether the moves were ever theirs.
