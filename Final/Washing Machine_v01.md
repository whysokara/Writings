Washing Machine

Why do washing machine minutes never feel like real minutes?

They're not.

The "1 minute remaining" on the display isn't a countdown. It's a social experiment to see how long you'll stand there, sock in hand, staring at wet clothes like staring will speed anything up.

Time in there is genuinely relative, depending on where you're standing. Inside the machine, 5 minutes is exactly 5 minutes. Outside, watching, 5 minutes stretches into something closer to the last over of a close T20 chase.. every second refusing to end. The machine knows this. It just doesn't care.

And the number lies differently depending on the day too. 5 minutes left can take 9 on a Saturday and 16 on a Monday, for reasons no engineer has ever bothered explaining. Washing machine minutes don't run on your clock. They run on their own timezone, somewhere your phone can't reach.

The number on the screen isn't a promise: it's a polite suggestion, made by a machine that will, 4 minutes after telling you it's done, decide it actually needs one more rinse. Or a longer spin. Or just 12 extra minutes to sit there quietly, thinking about its life choices.

Maybe every appliance with a countdown is lying to you a little. The washing machine's just the one that never bothered pretending otherwise.
