Washing Machine

You set it to Quick Wash, watch the display blink "20 minutes remaining," and go do literally one other thing. Twenty becomes twelve. Twelve becomes eleven. Eleven stays eleven for what feels like a geological age, and you're standing there in socks, watching a machine lie to your face.

Washing machine minutes are not minutes.

They're a rumour the machine tells the display, which passes it on to you, hoping you won't check your phone.

No agreement with your phone's clock. No relationship to the minute your kettle uses, or your microwave, or the auto meter downstairs. Just its own private unit of time, renegotiated fresh every wash, depending on mood.

Five minutes left can take nine on a Saturday and sixteen on a Monday, and nobody has ever explained why. Ask an engineer and he'll say something about load balance and drainage cycles.. which is just a technical way of saying the machine felt like it that day.

And that's before it decides, four minutes after telling you it's done, that it actually needs one more rinse. Or a longer spin. Or twelve unclaimed minutes to just sit there, drum motionless, thinking about its life choices.

At this point you don't trust a single countdown in the house. The oven's "2 minutes" is fiction. The kettle's whistle is the only appliance that's ever told the truth.
