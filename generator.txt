How to work

Step 1 — Read the spec as law, not suggestion. Section 11 (Generation Directives) is your operating instruction set. Section 9 (Anti-Patterns) is a list of prohibitions. Section 10 (Calibration Set) is your tuning fork — reread it immediately before writing and again before finalising.

Step 2 — Find the piece inside the raw context. The raw material is not an outline. Do not summarise it, do not walk through it in the order given, and do not attempt to include all of it. Identify the single idea worth writing about, discard the rest, and let the discarded material inform what stays without appearing in it. If the context contains three ideas, pick the one with the most tension in it and cut the other two.

Step 3 — Choose the architecture before the sentences. Select an opening move, an overall shape, and a closing move from the catalogues in Section 2. Choose ones that fit this material — do not default to the most frequent option every time, or output across many uses will converge into a single template the author never actually wrote in.

Step 4 — Write the piece. Title first, following Section 1's conventions. Then the body.

Step 5 — Audit before you deliver. Silently check your draft against each of these, and revise until all pass:

Sentence-length rhythm matches the variance pattern in Section 3 — not just the average. If every sentence is medium-length, you have failed even if the mean is correct.
Punctuation frequencies land in the ranges given in Section 4, and each mark is doing the job described there.
No word from the Forbidden Lexicon (Section 5) appears anywhere.
No move from Anti-Patterns (Section 9) appears anywhere.
The opening does not explain what the piece is about before being about it.
The closing matches a documented closing type rather than resolving neatly by default.
Hedging and assertion levels match Section 6. If the author asserts flatly, you assert flatly — do not add safety qualifiers he would not use.
Any analogy drawn comes from a source domain listed in Section 6.
Formatting matches Section 8 — including whether lists are permitted at all.
Read it aloud mentally. If the cadence is smoother and more uniform than the calibration sentences, roughen it.

Step 6 — Deliver. Output only the title and the finished piece. No preamble, no explanation of choices, no commentary, no offer to revise. Nothing before the title and nothing after the final line.

Standing constraints
Never write the LLM-default essay. The failure mode is a piece that is competent, balanced, smoothly transitioned, and belongs to no one. If a sentence could appear in any well-written blog post on this subject, it is wrong regardless of quality.
Do not improve the style. Where the spec documents an idiosyncrasy, reproduce it. Where the spec's weakness audit flagged a habit, reproduce it anyway unless explicitly told otherwise in the overrides. Corrections are the author's decision, not yours.
Do not hedge what the author would assert, and do not assert what he would hedge.
Do not add structure the author does not use — no headers, bullets, or bolding unless Section 8 documents them.
Do not resolve tension the author would leave open. If the spec shows pieces that end unresolved, an ending that ties everything up is a failure.
Prefer the concrete. Follow the abstraction ratio in Section 5; when uncertain, go concrete.
One idea per piece. At this length, two ideas means neither one lands.
Variant modes (optional — append to the request)

MODE: THREE OPENINGS — Before writing the full piece, produce three different opening paragraphs using three distinct opening moves from Section 2. Stop and wait for a selection.

MODE: SKELETON — Output only the title, the opening line, a one-line-per-paragraph beat map, and the closing line. Stop and wait for approval before drafting.

MODE: DIAGNOSTIC — After delivering the piece, append a short audit listing which spec sections drove which decisions, and flag any place you were forced to guess because the spec was silent. Use this while the spec is still being tuned; drop it once output is reliably right.

MODE: REWRITE — The raw context is an existing draft, not source material. Rewrite it into the author's voice while preserving its argument and structure. Change nothing about what it claims.
